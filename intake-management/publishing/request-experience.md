# The request experience

Most employees interact with Zip a handful of times a year. The experience has to make sense to someone who has forgotten everything since last time.

## The landing page

The landing page is where a requester starts. It has three jobs.

**Offer a clear starting point.** A small number of tiles, named for what someone wants to do: buy something new, renew an existing vendor, ask a question. Fewer tiles is better; the form's category question can handle the detail.

**Show existing work.** The requester's open requests, with current status and who is holding them, so they do not raise a duplicate or email procurement to ask.

**Answer questions before they become requests.** Link to your buying policy, your preferred vendor list, and the relevant parts of the [Help center](https://app.gitbook.com/s/xKEqt4qa9BH9Mk6NAbiK/). Where the [AI Procurement Concierge](https://app.gitbook.com/s/cfyHKXX03HFOzcl9ZWBb/) is enabled, requesters can ask in plain language and be pointed at the right entry point.

## Filling in the form

Requesters do not fill forms in one sitting. Two behaviors matter:

**Drafts save automatically.** A requester who leaves mid-form finds their answers when they return.

**The form shortens as it goes.** Conditional logic means answering the category question removes most of the remaining questions. Requesters read this as the form being short, which it is, for them.

{% hint style="info" %}
Requesters submit better information when they know why they are being asked. Use descriptive text under the questions reviewers care about: "Legal needs this to confirm whether the agreement can use our paper."
{% endhint %}

## After submission

Once submitted, the request opens on its own page. The requester sees:

- The current status and the step it is on.
- Who is currently assigned, by name, not by team.
- What has already been approved, with timestamps.
- A comment thread for questions from reviewers.

Notifications reach requesters by email and, where configured, in Slack or Microsoft Teams. Approvers can act directly from those messages, which is the single biggest lever on cycle time.

## Responding to a request for information

When a reviewer needs something more, they send the request back for information rather than rejecting it. The requester gets a notification, the request shows what is needed, and answering returns it to the same step rather than restarting the chain.

## Withdrawing and duplicating

A requester can withdraw their own request at any point before it becomes a purchase order. Withdrawn requests stay visible in history.

Duplicating an existing request pre-fills a new one with the previous answers. This is the normal path for renewals, and it is worth telling requesters about: it turns a fifteen-minute form into a two-minute one.

## What to measure

Watch the abandonment rate on your forms and the time requesters spend on each section. Both are available in [Spend insights](https://app.gitbook.com/s/zebllmmpY7BlosLYBwUh/). A section with a high abandonment rate is usually asking for something the requester does not have.
