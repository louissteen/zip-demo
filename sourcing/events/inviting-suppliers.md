# Invite suppliers

Suppliers do not need an existing vendor record to bid. They need an email address and a way into the event. Onboarding happens after award, not before, so you are not asking eight suppliers to complete tax forms for a contract seven of them will not win.

{% stepper %}
{% step %}
## Build the invitation list

On the event, open **Suppliers** and add participants. Search existing vendors by name or category, or add a new supplier with a company name and contact email. You can import a list from a file.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
## Set the primary contact

Each supplier needs one primary contact who receives the invitation and owns the response. Additional contacts can be added by the supplier once it accepts.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
## Send the invitations

Select **Send invitations**. Each supplier receives an email with the event summary, the deadline, and a link. Suppliers respond in the [vendor portal](https://app.gitbook.com/s/6BeW2bup2FUvVioEI5Le/), creating an account on first use.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
## Track acceptance

The **Suppliers** tab shows who has been invited, who has viewed the event, who has declined, and whose response is in progress or submitted. Follow up on suppliers that have not opened the invitation a few days before the deadline.
{% endstep %}
{% endstepper %}

## The clarification period

Suppliers ask questions through the event rather than by emailing the buyer. That is deliberate: it keeps the record complete and prevents one supplier getting information the others do not have.

Questions arrive in the **Q and A** tab. You can answer a question privately when the answer is specific to one supplier's circumstances, but the default and the safer choice is to publish the answer to everyone. Published answers are visible to all invited suppliers with the original question anonymized.

{% hint style="warning" %}
Answering a substantive question privately is the most common way a competitive process becomes challengeable. If the answer changes how a supplier would price or scope, publish it and consider extending the deadline.
{% endhint %}

## Extending, withdrawing, and adding late

You can extend the deadline at any point before the event closes. Every invited supplier is notified.

A supplier can decline. Recording the reason is useful, because a pattern of declines in a category tells you something about your specifications or your terms.

Adding a supplier after the event opens is possible but should be rare. Late entrants have less time, and if they win, the fairness of the process is harder to defend. Zip records the invitation date for every supplier so the gap is visible.

## Existing vendor data

When you invite a supplier that already has a vendor record, Zip shows what it already knows: current contracts, risk tier, and past event performance. That context is available to evaluators alongside the bid, so an incumbent's history is part of the comparison rather than folklore.

For how supplier records are created and maintained, see [Vendor Management](https://app.gitbook.com/s/VzFAfjeuJK2DKd9GcLgy/).
