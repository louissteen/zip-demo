# Savings tracking

Savings are only credible if the baseline is. Zip records the baseline at the point of negotiation, when everyone still remembers what the alternative was, rather than reconstructing it at quarter end.

## Baseline types

The baseline is what you would have paid without the work. Which baseline applies depends on the situation.

**Prior price.** The rate on the expiring contract, for renewals. The most defensible baseline, because it is a number you actually paid.

**Initial quote.** The supplier's first offer, for new purchases with no history. Reasonable when the quote was genuine, weak when the supplier opened high in anticipation of negotiation.

**Average of competing bids.** The mean of qualified bids in a sourcing event. Useful where no incumbent price exists.

**Budgeted amount.** What was approved to be spent. This measures budget performance rather than negotiation, so treat it as a separate category.

{% hint style="warning" %}
Do not use the highest bid as the baseline. It inflates reported savings and it is the first thing a skeptical finance partner will challenge.
{% endhint %}

## Savings categories

Zip separates savings into categories because finance treats them differently.

**Hard savings.** A reduction against a price you are currently paying. These reduce future spend and can be taken to budget.

**Cost avoidance.** A reduction against a price you would have paid but never did, such as negotiating down an opening quote or removing an announced uplift. Real value, but no line in the budget goes down.

**Non-financial value.** Better payment terms, extended warranty, added scope at the same price, or reduced risk exposure. Recorded so the negotiation is not judged on price alone.

## Recording savings

Savings are recorded on the sourcing event or on the linked intake request. Enter the baseline amount, the baseline type, the final amount, the currency, the period the savings covers, and the category. Attach the evidence, usually the prior contract or the opening quote.

Savings above a threshold can be routed for finance validation before they count in reporting. Validation is a workflow step like any other, and the validator can accept, adjust, or reject the claim with a comment.

## Realized against forecast

Recording a saving forecasts it. Realizing it depends on what actually gets spent. Zip compares the forecast against actual spend on the resulting purchase orders and bills over the savings period.

A negotiated unit-price reduction on a contract nobody uses realizes nothing. Reporting shows both numbers so the difference is visible rather than assumed away.

For spend analysis, category reporting, and savings dashboards across all events, see [Spend Insights](https://app.gitbook.com/s/zebllmmpY7BlosLYBwUh/).

<details>

<summary>How are multi-year savings counted?</summary>

Enter the annualized saving and the term. Zip reports the annual figure in each period it applies to rather than recognizing the full multi-year value in the year of the negotiation. Most finance teams require this, and it prevents a single large deal distorting a year of reporting.

</details>
