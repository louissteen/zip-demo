# Savings tracking

Savings are claimed by procurement and validated by finance. The gap between the two is almost always about definition and evidence, not arithmetic. Recording savings on the request, at the moment they are negotiated, closes most of that gap.

## Classify before you count

Use a small, fixed set of savings types and apply them consistently.

**Hard savings** reduce spend against a comparable prior baseline. A renewal negotiated below last year's price is a hard saving. These reduce budget and finance will generally accept them.

**Cost avoidance** reduces spend against a quoted or proposed price that was never paid. A vendor's opening quote negotiated down is cost avoidance. It does not reduce budget, and it should never be presented as if it does.

**Value gained** captures non-price outcomes: additional licenses at the same price, extended term, improved payment terms, added support. Record the commercial value and the basis.

**Compliance savings** come from redirecting spend to an existing contract or preferred vendor.

{% hint style="warning" %}
Reporting cost avoidance and hard savings as one number is the fastest way to lose finance's confidence in the whole figure. Report them separately, always, even when the combined total looks better.
{% endhint %}

## Recording a saving

Savings are recorded on the request or the sourcing event that produced them. Capture five things:

- The type, from the fixed list.
- The baseline, and where the baseline came from: the prior contract, the vendor's initial quote, a benchmark, a competing bid.
- The final negotiated amount.
- The period over which the saving applies, so a multi-year outcome is not counted once at full value.
- The owner who negotiated it.

Attach the evidence. A quote, a redline, or a prior contract stored on the request is what makes the figure defensible twelve months later.

## Baselines

The baseline is where savings claims fail review. Two rules keep them defensible.

**Prefer a documented baseline over an inferred one.** A prior invoice or an executed contract beats an estimate.

**Do not use the vendor's opening ask as a hard savings baseline.** Vendors set opening asks knowing they will be negotiated. That is cost avoidance, and calling it anything else is what causes the argument.

## Annualizing

A multi-year agreement negotiated below baseline saves over the whole term, not in one period. Record the annual value and the term, and report the annualized figure. Counting the full term value in the year of signature inflates the year and creates a hole in the next.

## Reporting

The savings dashboard groups by type, owner, category, and period. Two views matter:

**By type**, for finance, so hard savings and cost avoidance are visibly separate.

**By owner and category**, for the procurement team, so credit is attributable and coverage gaps are visible.

Where savings come out of a competitive process, the event detail in [Sourcing](https://app.gitbook.com/s/yWPKTXf10NGJgEVmE6Ok/) provides the bid comparison that supports the baseline.

## Reviewing with finance

Set a quarterly review where finance samples recorded savings and checks the evidence. Sampling a handful of entries a quarter is far less work than defending a year's total at once, and it keeps the definitions from drifting.
