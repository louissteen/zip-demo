# View purchase orders

A purchase order is your customer's commitment to buy. It carries a PO number, the goods or services, the amounts, and the terms. You need the PO number to invoice, so start here.

## Reading a purchase order

Open a PO from the **Purchase orders** page. It shows:

**Header.** PO number, issue date, the customer entity buying from you, the buyer contact, the currency, and the payment terms.

**Line items.** Each item with its description, quantity, unit price, and total. Invoice against these lines, and use the same descriptions your customer used.

**Delivery and billing.** Where goods go, where the invoice should be addressed, and any delivery dates.

**Invoiced to date.** How much of the PO you have already invoiced and how much remains. On a blanket PO covering a period, this is the figure to watch.

**Attachments and terms.** Any documents your customer attached, and the terms that apply to the order.

## Downloading and acknowledging

Select **Download** for a PDF copy for your own records or to attach to your order system.

If your customer requires acknowledgement, an **Acknowledge** button appears. Acknowledging confirms you accept the order as written. If something is wrong, do not acknowledge; contact the buyer named on the PO and ask for a revision.

{% hint style="warning" %}
Do not begin work or ship goods against a verbal instruction while waiting for a PO. Invoices without a matching PO are rejected by most customers using Zip, and the resulting dispute is resolved slowly. If you have been asked to start urgently, ask for the PO number in writing first.
{% endhint %}

## Standard and blanket purchase orders

A **standard PO** covers a specific purchase and is typically invoiced once or in a few known stages.

A **blanket PO** sets a spending ceiling over a period, and you invoice against it repeatedly, most often monthly. Each invoice reduces the remaining balance. When the balance runs low, tell the buyer before you invoice past it rather than after, because an invoice exceeding the remaining balance will not be paid until the PO is increased.

## Revisions

If your customer changes a PO, you receive a revised version and a notification. The revision number increases and the previous version stays visible on the record. Always invoice against the current version.

## Closed purchase orders

A PO is closed when it has been fully received and invoiced, or when the buyer closes it. You cannot invoice against a closed PO. If you have a legitimate invoice for a closed PO, contact the buyer and ask them to reopen it or issue a new one.

<details>

<summary>I received goods requests but no PO.</summary>

Ask the person who requested the work for the PO number. In Zip, a purchase order exists only after the request has been approved internally, so a missing PO usually means approval is still in progress. Starting work before then is at your own risk.

</details>

<details>

<summary>The PO is addressed to the wrong entity of ours.</summary>

Contact the buyer and ask for a revision. The entity on the PO must match the entity on your invoice and the entity holding the bank account, or the invoice will fail matching.

</details>
