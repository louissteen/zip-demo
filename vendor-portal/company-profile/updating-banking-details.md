# Update banking details

Change your bank details in the portal yourself. Your customer will not act on bank details sent by email, and expects the change to come from your own signed-in account.

{% stepper %}
{% step %}
## Open your company profile

Sign in, go to **Company profile**, and open **Bank accounts**. You will see the accounts currently on file for this customer.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
## Add the new account

Select **Add account** and enter the details for the country and currency: account holder name, account number or IBAN, the routing identifier, and the bank name and address. The account holder name must match your registered legal entity.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
## Upload verification evidence

Attach a bank letter, a voided check, or a statement header showing the account holder name and the account number. Your customer uses this to confirm the account is yours.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
## Complete the verification step

Depending on your customer's process, you may also confirm a small test deposit in the portal, or take a phone call from your customer's finance team on a number they source independently. Expect this and answer promptly.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
## Wait for approval

Your customer reviews and approves the change. Scheduled payments to you are held while the change is pending, which is deliberate: it prevents a payment going out to an account nobody has verified yet.
{% endstep %}

{% step %}
## Confirm the change took effect

Once approved, the new account shows as active on your profile. Check it before your next invoice is paid.
{% endstep %}
{% endstepper %}

{% hint style="danger" %}
If you receive a message claiming to be from your customer asking you to confirm or change your bank details outside the portal, do not respond to it. Sign in to the portal directly, and report the message to your customer using a contact you already have. The same fraud runs in the opposite direction, so expect your customer to call you to verify a change you genuinely made.
{% endhint %}

## Timing

Submit a bank change well before an invoice is due. Verification takes time, particularly where a test deposit or a phone call is involved, and payments are held while the change is pending. Changing details the week a large payment is due delays that payment.

## Multiple accounts

You can hold more than one account where you invoice a customer in more than one currency, or where different entities of yours are paid separately. Each account is linked to its currency and entity, and your customer selects the right one automatically.

## Who can make the change

Only contacts with the finance role on your company profile can view or change bank details. Other contacts do not see the account numbers. Review who holds that role periodically, and remove people who have left. See [Manage contacts](managing-contacts.md).

<details>

<summary>Do I need to do this separately for each customer?</summary>

Yes. Each customer holds its own record of your details and runs its own verification. A change with one customer does not propagate to another, which is a control rather than an inconvenience.

</details>

<details>

<summary>Our bank has changed our account number.</summary>

Add the new account rather than editing the existing one, and tell your customer the date the old account closes. Editing in place makes it harder to reconcile payments that were in flight when the change happened.

</details>
